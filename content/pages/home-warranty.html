Home warranty service, start to finish

How a warranty call works, who decides what, and what happens when something isn't covered. Your plan's contract has the final word, but this is how it usually goes.

How a warranty service call works

  1. You request service from your warranty company. Online or by phone, as soon as you notice the problem. Your plan sets a service fee (often called a trade call fee) for each request.
  2. Your warranty company sends a contractor. The warranty company chooses which contractor to send. If they send Kappa-Air, we'll call you to set up a visit during business hours.
  3. We diagnose the problem. We inspect the system, find the cause, and explain what we found in plain language.
  4. We report to your warranty company. We send them our diagnosis and what it will take to fix it: the equipment's model and serial numbers, the failed part, and the repair or replacement we recommend.
  5. Your warranty company decides and approves. They review our findings against your plan and decide what's covered. We don't start covered work until they approve it.
  6. We do the approved work. We order parts or equipment and complete the repair or replacement your warranty company approved.

Repairs

Most warranty calls end with a repair. We work on central air conditioners, heat pumps, gas and electric furnaces, air handlers, and package units, and we repair all major brands, including Carrier, Bryant, Trane, Rheem, Goodman, American Standard, Payne, Lennox, York, and Amana.

Common problems we diagnose: failed capacitors and contactors, fan motors, refrigerant leaks, clogged condensate drains, dirty or damaged coils, failed compressors, thermostat and control problems, and heating systems that short-cycle or won't light.

Replacements: we recommend, your warranty company decides

When a system can't be repaired, or a repair doesn't make sense, we'll tell your warranty company that we recommend replacing it, and why. The decision belongs to your warranty company. Warranty contracts give the warranty company the right to decide whether a covered item is repaired or replaced, and they approve the work every step of the way.

  • Nothing starts without approval. We install only what your warranty company has authorized. If we find something new partway through the job, we stop and get it approved before going further.
  • Like for like. Warranty replacements generally match your old system's capacity, efficiency, and features. They don't have to match its brand, color, or exact size.
  • Plans have limits. Most plans cap what they'll pay for a heating or cooling system. In some cases a warranty company may offer, or choose, a cash payment instead of a replacement. That's between you and your warranty company.
  • Newer refrigerants can mean more work. Today's systems use newer refrigerants, so replacing an outdoor unit can also mean replacing the indoor coil or the refrigerant lines to match. Some plans cover this and some don't.

Whatever your warranty company approves, we install it properly, sized and set up the way the manufacturer intends.

What if something isn't covered?

Home warranties cover a lot, but not everything. Depending on your plan, homeowners are commonly responsible for things like:

  • Permits and code upgrades required to do the work
  • Changes needed to fit new equipment: ductwork, plenums, electrical, or refrigerant lines
  • Cranes or lifting equipment for rooftop units
  • Refrigerant beyond your plan's limit
  • Hauling away old equipment
  • Costs above your plan's dollar limit
  • Items your plan excludes, such as air purifiers, humidifiers, UV lights, and window units
  • Problems caused by missing maintenance, improper installation, or earlier modifications

If we find something your plan doesn't cover, we'll still tell you what we'd recommend and why, and we'll give you the price before we do anything. Whether to go ahead is completely up to you. Non-covered work is paid to us directly, and we never start it without your OK.

Have a question about a coverage decision? Your warranty company is the place to ask. Many plans include a way to request a second opinion if you disagree with a decision.

Keep your system, and your coverage, in good shape

Most warranty contracts make routine maintenance the homeowner's job, and problems caused by skipped maintenance usually aren't covered. A little care goes a long way:

  • Change your air filter regularly, more often in summer and during dust season.
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of plants, weeds, and debris.
  • Have the system tuned up once a year, ideally twice: AC in spring, heat in fall.
  • Keep your maintenance receipts. They help if there's ever a question about a claim.

Some plans include seasonal tune-ups; check with your warranty company. Either way, we're happy to do your maintenance. Call {{PHONE}}.

Tips for a smooth claim

  • Request service right away. Plans require you to report problems promptly, and waiting can make the damage worse.
  • Keep your plan handy. It spells out your coverage, limits, and service fee.
  • Know your equipment. The brand and model number from the labels on your indoor and outdoor units help everyone move faster.
  • Describe the symptoms. Warm air, strange noises, water leaks, or a system that won't turn on: the more detail, the better prepared we arrive.
  • Can I ask for Kappa-Air? Usually your warranty company chooses the contractor. Some plans let you request one, or use a contractor outside their network with approval first, so it's worth asking.
